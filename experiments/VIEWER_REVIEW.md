# Viewer review for MinuteMe GS visual experiments

Do not call a reel 10/10 or predict shares/retention from a successful render.
Before presenting a final preview, watch/inspect the exported video, with the same WhatsApp panel and layout used for publication.

Record:
- Who this specific reel is for, and why its first image/question matters to them.
- The single idea a viewer can explain after watching.
- What the animation demonstrates that the narration alone does not.
- What new information each scene adds; reject repeated scenes with only changed headings.
- A concrete reason someone might send it to a specific person. A generic share CTA is insufficient.
- Why the next episode would be worth following for.
- Whether the final WhatsApp/notes panel is readable outside interface overlays and preserves teaching space.
- Factual source and whether the scene simplifies or exaggerates a process.

Acknowledge weak or unsupported areas. A score is editorial opinion, not audience evidence.
A rejected export stays on publication hold. Preserve regular 7 PYQ and MEGA scheduling; do not silently substitute rejected visuals.
Compare actual audience results at matched ages before selecting a winning format.


## User correction: preserve the cinematic reference quality

The user's reference is the published Atacama/Humboldt reel built with polished_gs.py: shaded rotating globe, real Natural Earth geography, full-frame layered scenes, large colored Hindi titles and moving causal paths. This is the visual baseline for new cinematic experiments. viewer_seabreeze_story.py and the simple cartoon diagrams were rejected as a quality regression; do not use them as the default just because they render quickly. Keep explanations simple without reducing illustration quality. Use globe/map context only when it contributes geographic meaning. ## Applies to every subject: choose the visual for the idea

The globe is a quality reference, NOT a mandatory motif. Never repeat Earth, maps, actors or decorative camera moves when they do not explain the topic. Apply the cinematic visual-quality target across science, polity, geography, history, economics, static GK and current affairs, and choose the right medium for each idea.

- Science: topic-specific moving mechanisms, forces, heat, circuits, fluids, body models or experiments; make the changed quantity visible.
- Geography: accurate regional maps, terrain, rivers, ocean/air movement and cross-sections; globe only for geographic context that is needed.
- History: source-grounded maps, movement between places, artifacts, chronological events and causal transitions; no invented reconstructions presented as evidence.
- Polity: clearly distinguish institutions, roles and a proposal/document moving through a verified process; counters or diagrams only when the quantities/process are sourced.
- Economics: show goods, money, quantities, prices and exchanges changing together; graphs must have correct axes and grounded values, not invented statistics.
- Static GK and current affairs: a recognizable real object/place/event, followed by a relevant spatial, functional or comparison animation. Verify contemporary facts before illustration.

Art direction comes before choosing a renderer. For each reel state the single learning goal, the objects needed, what visibly changes in each shot, and why that movement helps understanding. Prefer large subject-specific visual scenes, depth/lighting where meaningful, legible color-coded Hindi, restrained sound and camera motion, and narration synchronized with the mechanism. Easy language must not mean low-quality imagery. More motion is not automatically better: remove it when it adds confusion.

A new module or a written direction does not establish that every subject renderer is implemented. Record unsupported visual needs explicitly; do not claim universal high-quality automation from a prompt or a passing build. Preserve factual correctness, the visible safe-area WhatsApp/notes panel, and any existing publication holds.
